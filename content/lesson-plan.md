# EFL Lesson Plan: Exploring Cultural Myths and Legends

**Target Audience:** Intermediate (B1-B2) English/Spanish as a Foreign Language learners.
**Duration:** 90 minutes
**Theoretical Framework:** Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) & Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT)

---

## Lesson Objectives

-   **Linguistic:** Students will be able to use narrative tenses (past simple, past continuous, past perfect) to retell events and describe characters.
-   **Communicative:** Students will collaborate in groups to reconstruct a myth from scattered information and present it to the class.
-   **Cultural:** Students will analyze and discuss the moral or cultural significance embedded in traditional myths.

---

## Materials Needed

-   Short texts of 3 different myths/legends (e.g., La Llorona, King Arthur, The Monkey King).
-   Vocabulary flashcards related to mythology (e.g., hero, quest, curse, supernatural).
-   Large paper and markers for group presentations.
-   Audio/Video clip of a storyteller narrating a short legend.

---

## Procedure (TBLT Framework)

### 1. Pre-Task Phase (15 mins)

**Goal:** Activate schema and introduce the topic.

**Activity:** _Brainstorming & Input._ The teacher asks students what makes a story a "myth" or a "legend." Students discuss briefly in pairs. The teacher then plays a short, engaging audio clip of a well-known legend. After listening, the class identifies key elements (heroes, magic, moral lessons). Key vocabulary is introduced using flashcards.

### 2. Task Phase (40 mins)

**Goal:** Meaningful communication and collaboration.

**Activity:** _The Jigsaw Myth Reconstruction._

-   Students are divided into small groups.
-   Each group is given a set of mixed-up sentence strips or paragraphs belonging to one specific myth.
-   The group must work together to sequence the story correctly, discussing the logical flow of events and using transition words.
-   Once sequenced, they prepare a dramatic retelling of the story to present to the class. They must focus on conveying the meaning and emotion of the story rather than perfect accuracy.

### 3. Post-Task / Planning & Report Phase (20 mins)

**Goal:** Presentation and peer feedback.

**Activity:** _Storytelling Time._ Each group presents their reconstructed myth to the class. The audience listens actively to identify the main moral or cultural lesson of the story presented. After each presentation, a brief Q&A session allows peers to ask clarification questions.

### 4. Language Focus Phase (15 mins)

**Goal:** Explicit attention to language forms used during the task.

**Activity:** _Reviewing Narrative Tenses._ The teacher highlights effective uses of narrative tenses observed during the presentations. The teacher pulls specific sentences from the original myth texts to analyze the difference between past simple (main events), past continuous (background actions), and past perfect (actions before other past actions). Students complete a short, guided practice exercise.

---

## Theoretical Justification

This lesson plan heavily relies on **Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)** by prioritizing meaning-making and authentic communication over rote memorization. The "Jigsaw Myth Reconstruction" is a core element of **Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT)**, where language is a tool used to solve a problem (sequencing the story) rather than the primary object of study initially. The focus on language forms (grammar) occurs only _after_ the communicative task has been completed, adhering to the TBLT principle of moving from fluency to accuracy.
